The two states

The console out of combat, the Halo in it, and what rides on them.

State
World and combat states. After a fight the console opens again once things have been calm for 1, 3, 5 (default) or 8 seconds; in a group it also waits while anyone near you is still fighting (up to 20 seconds), so a chain of pulls stays one fight. In combat (Settings: In combat) the console folds while the Halo rises (default), stays open with the Halo, or stays open with no Halo; it only folds when the Halo is on, with or without the slide.
Console
Out of combat, on a tooled leather panel whose lines take your theme accent: portrait medallion (click to target yourself) with health and mana either side, and a Ready, Journey and Company page. Ready draws your most worn piece of gear as ten cells and your bags as one small column each, with the free count beside them; Journey draws experience with your rest in blue behind it and an accent mark where an hour at your pace would take you. Holds chat on its left page, with leather tabs standing on its top edge, and keeps bags and the game menu in its bottom-right corner. Strips the action bar art. Folds down flat in combat. On a narrow screen or a large UI scale the pages keep clear of health, mana and the action bars, dropping a column (Company first) rather than overlapping, and the chat wraps sooner.
Halo
In combat: health and mana arcs, signature spell with cooldown, target and cast bar around your character.
Roles
Maps each dual-spec slot to a role; set it in settings or use the class default.
Per spec
Combat rows, the click grid, the bar tray and At a glance keep their own setting in each spec, so a healing spec and a damage spec each look right without changing anything.
Combat bar
In combat, a compact row of your main bar's spells under the Halo: rounded icons, the key small in each one's top corner (⇧ for Shift), cooldown sweeps; dimmed when unusable, blue when short of mana, faded with its key in the warning colour when out of range. Up to two more rows follow from the bottom-left and bottom-right bars, the same size and on the same columns (Settings: Combat rows; healers default to one). Spells on a long cooldown sit dim until they're ready.
Coin art
Each Halo coin carries its spell's icon faintly behind the letter, in colour while it runs or is ready and grey while it's off or coming back (a setting)
Procs
The game's own proc art either side of the Halo and its proc glow on the combat bar button, from the game's proc events and from STOA's own reading (usable, free or faster casts) while buffs are hidden. Procs nothing readable changes (Redoubt, Vengeance, Martyrdom...) are small game-drawn coins above the Halo. Art found by the game's file ID; STOA's own thin arc if a picture is missing; /stoa procs test, /stoa procs list.
Game auras
Forever's aura container draws chosen buffs and debuffs with real timers even in combat; /stoa auras checks it.
Reminders
Missing self-buffs as words: aura, seal and Righteous Fury for paladins, Inner Fire and Shadowform for priests. Under the Halo in combat, beside your role on the console out of it.
Mana play
For every mana user: a small spark runs along the Halo's mana arc and the console's mana bar while the five-second rule holds back spirit regeneration, then pulses on each regeneration tick; while you cast, the mana the spell will take is shaded; druids in cat or bear form see their mana as a slim bar under the power number. Each has its own setting.
Swing timer
Blizzard's swing timer (place it in Edit Mode) as a flat bar in the nameplates' rounded dark track: pale gold for the main hand, blue for the off hand, bone for ranged; amber words when out of range.
Threat meter
Up to five people by threat on your target, or your tank's, with the tank first and marked TANK, to the left of the combat bar, in the damage meters' style. In combat in a group by default. Non-tanks see PULLING AGGRO with the percent on the target plaque at 90%.
Timers
A slim bar near the top: flights show where you're going and the time left, from known flight times (Data/FlightTimes.lua) or timed the first time you fly a route and remembered; the flight map's tooltips show the time. Breath, fatigue and feign death use the same bar, worded, in place of Blizzard's. /stoa timers shows a sample.
Cooldown Manager
Off for now (Settings > Combat > Cooldowns). Halo coins ask the game's Cooldown Manager whether a spell is ready or a buff is up, which it knows in combat; its rows keep running out of sight. Spells it doesn't track keep STOA's own reading. /stoa cdm.

Groups

Party and raid frames, healing and the people you play with.

Party cards
In Slim and Minimal, tanks and damage dealers see thin strips to the left of the Halo: a name, health and a hairline bar, one word when you can act, faint while everyone is fine (Settings: Setup, Party when not healing). Otherwise cards: name and health percent, a thick health bar with shields on its end, and word pips for your buffs, Weakened Soul, dispels and low health. Rounded cards. A row under the Halo when you heal, with a grid of your click bindings on the left; a column on the left otherwise.
Raid frames
When you heal, your group reads as rows right under the Halo at every size, with the combat bar below them: ten players are two rows of party cards, twenty are compact rows of small cells with health filling the cell behind the name and one word (MAG, DIS, LOW, DEAD) in place of the percent; forty are two blocks of smaller cells side by side, groups 1-4 and 5-8. Otherwise one column per group on the left. /stoa test raid, raid10 and raid40 preview them.
Click-casting
Fifteen bindings per role on party and raid cards: five mouse buttons, plain, Shift and Ctrl. /stoa clicks to edit. When you heal, a grid beside the cards spells out each click; by default it folds to its title and opens while you hover a card or hold Shift. Click its title to keep it open, or set it in settings or with /stoa grid.
Tank watch
For healers, a gold-lined leather plaque with a portrait coin at the Halo's upper left in combat: your focus or the group's tank, with a health word, shields and your buffs on them. Your click-casting bindings work on it.
Target
A solid plaque on the Halo's right with a portrait (in Slim and Minimal, words and a bar over the world with a small portrait; Settings: Setup, Target), level and kind, and an engraved bar; elite gets a doubled gold line and rare a silver one, each with the word. For any target, in or out of combat (a dead one dims and says DEAD): name, health, a HOSTILE cue, threat in words and auras. Right click for the unit menu.
Pet
For Hunters, Warlocks and any pet: in a fight a pet plaque like the target's at the Halo's upper left (mood HAPPY, CONTENT or UNHAPPY, health, focus or mana, stance and what it's on), a thin inner arc on the Halo with its health and a smaller pet row under the combat bar (right-click for autocast). Out of a fight a pet coin hangs off the medallion with its health as a ring and its mood; click it to feed it from your bags. Reminders: CALL PET, PET DEAD · REVIVE, FEED <NAME>, PET LOW, PET PASSIVE and GROWL ON in a group. Healers in a party see a slim cell under each owner's card.
Group buffs
Out of combat, the console's Ready page says who in range is missing a buff you can cast (FORTITUDE · 3 missing; any blessing counts for paladins), and the same line shows during a ready check.
Dungeon kit
Journey shows experience an hour and time to level beside a small line chart of your pace (worked out over the last half hour, started again at each level, hidden at the highest level) and, when you are alone, the progress of the nearest watched quest. /stoa pull is a countdown: the game's own when you can use it, otherwise 3, 2, 1 in group chat and a large number on screen. A ready check dresses the prompt in a STOA panel and puts READY, NOT READY or WAITING on each party card. The target plaque says KICK while the target casts something you can interrupt. Each has its own setting.
Social
Accept invites from friends and guild; decline duels, party and guild invites from strangers.
Auto-invite
Friends and guildmates who whisper inv (in game or over Battle.net) get invited; optionally the keyword works in guild chat. Says so when the group is full or you aren't leader.
Company
/stoa friends or a click on the console's Company page: online friends, Battle.net friends in this game, and guild, with level, zone and AWAY/BUSY. Click to whisper, Shift- or right-click to invite. The console names a few friends online when you're alone.

Around the screen

Everything else the game draws, in STOA's dress.

Action bars
Rounded-square icons with a hairline edge like the party cards, dim sockets for empty slots, STOA type on keys and counts. Out of combat every console row shows; in combat the bars step aside for the combat bar (your keys still work), or every row shows if the combat bar is off.
At a glance
Up to eight numbers you choose, to the right of chat on the console (out of combat): stats (attack or spell power, healing, crit, hit, regen, armor, defense and more), gear wear, bag room, gold, professions, zone, watched reputation, time, latency and framerate, plus anything another addon publishes through LibDataBroker (the feeds Titan Panel shows). It follows where you are: one set out in the world (gold, gold an hour, loot, professions, reputation, gear, bags), one in a dungeon and one in a raid (your role's stats first), named in the accent beside its heading. Hover a line to see where the number comes from (stats by school, regen while casting and outside the five-second rule, your most worn items, bag by bag, gold and reputation this session), click it to open the window you'd go to next (character sheet, bags, currencies, professions, reputation, map, clock). Gear, reputation and professions carry a slim bar, bags a column per bag and gold an hour a small line chart. Right-click or /stoa glance to choose, per situation; a pinned line shows in every situation. Saved per character and spec, and until you choose it follows your role.
Minimap
A square with rounded corners like STOA's windows (or round, in settings), framed by an accent line and a hairline, or by a painted leather and brass frame (Ornament: Painted, which also frames the portrait coins and the Halo's signature slot); serif zone name, wheel to zoom.
Icon tray
Other addons' minimap buttons (LibDBIcon ones and any icon-sized button on the map that isn't Blizzard's or a map pin) gather into one small button below the map's left corner. Click it for a tray of their icons in sockets that follow the map's shape (round on a round map, rounded squares on a square one); it folds a moment after the mouse leaves. A setting turns it off; MinimapButtonButton and similar addons count as the same job.
Top of screen
Buffs as cropped icons in a hairline square with the time under them (pale gold when it runs short, amber edge for debuffs). Quest tracker headers in the display face over a hairline, bone quest titles, quiet minus and plus to fold. In combat the list folds to its header and comes back after; hover the header to open it (Settings: Fold quest list in combat). The zone above the minimap on a dark rounded panel with the clock beside it; tracking, calendar and the addon button wait for the mouse.
Nameplates
Blizzard's plates in a rounded dark track: amber for every enemy, pale gold for neutral, blue for friends, grey only for tapped or dead; enemy players in class colour. A bone edge on your target, the level as a plain number shaded by difficulty, cast bars on the same rounded ground. Styles: STOA, Bold (heavy frame, health value), Inline (name and health in the bar), Brass (brass edge, level badge), Slim. Target marked by edge, arrows or glow; other plates can fade, the target can grow The game's damage numbers over what you hit are drawn smaller (Settings: Damage numbers), so they cover less of the Halo when an enemy stands on you.
Chat
Web addresses in chat are links that open a box to copy them; a quiet copy mark (or Shift-click a chat tab, or /stoa copychat) shows the last 200 lines to copy; the main chat's last 100 lines come back, dimmed, after a reload or login. A time column on each line, more air between lines, the chat buttons in one quiet column and a quiet typing strip.
Map
The world map fades while you move, and a zone's level range shows beside the map title and on the minimap's zone tooltip (classic zones; English names for now)
Damage meters
Blizzard's meter windows with no panel, just outlined words and faint bars (your own in the accent, names in class colour), side by side in the top-left corner.

Windows

Blizzard's windows, kept and dressed.

Blizzard windows
Quest, gossip, merchant, trainer, bags and tooltips on dark rounded panels, with light quest text in STOA's type, dark rounded buttons, a quiet close cross and plain-word tabs.
Windows
Restyled windows and STOA's own pages share one build: 10px corners, a lighter header band down to where Blizzard's content starts (warm for trade windows, cool for social ones, plain for the rest) with the title seated in it and a soft shadow under it, a dark footer tray along the bottom, darker wells behind grouped things (quest rewards, text boxes), and tabs left where Blizzard hangs them, dressed as dark tabs tucked under the window with the chosen one underlined. Corner art is cut by Tools/art/window_corners.py.
Window stone
Tyler's stone art (faint rings and grain, Tools/art/source/wizard-stone2.png) in the body of every restyled window and STOA's own pages, tinted with the theme's text colour so it sits on any theme, and fading out towards the top where titles and text start; the console keeps its leather. A setting turns it off. The texture is cut by Tools/art/window_stone.py.
More windows
Spellbook, talents, world map border (never the map itself), mail, auction house, group finder, and friends and guild in the same dark panels, under the same embossed metal header that every Blizzard window wears (it rises a little above the window so the title has room) (the game menu and settings panel are left alone, to avoid blocked-action errors), dressed when each loads.
Moving windows
Hold Shift and drag a quest, gossip, merchant, bag, character, spellbook, mail, auction, world map (windowed) or similar window by its title or edge. Each keeps its spot whenever it opens again; Shift and right-click puts one back. Shift-clicking items still splits and links as usual.
Bags
Rounded item slots with a hairline edge; quality shown as an edge colour plus a corner mark for epic and above, item level on gear, free slots with any currencies you show on the backpack, money in g/s/c letters (hover it for every character's gold and the total), a Sort button, and a combined or separate layout option. The reagent bag opens with the combined bag as its bottom section, in the same ten columns (a setting)
Character sheet
A STOA panel, quality edges and item level on every equipment slot, WORN (under 30%) and BROKEN words for durability, an average item level line, and item levels in the inspect window.
Tooltips
Cards with a header band behind the name (room above and below it, a soft shadow under it), names in the serif and in the unit's colour (class, or the nameplates' reaction colours). The edge stays a plain hairline except for rank: elites get a doubled gold line, rares a doubled silver one, rare elites gold outside and silver inside, bosses a heavier doubled gold, and their header warms with that colour fading across it; items above common fade their quality colour across the header. For enemies, how hard they are as a word (TRIVIAL, EASY, EVEN, HARD, DEADLY, plus ELITE, RARE or BOSS) rather than the game's red-green level colours; whom they target (YOU in the warning colour), what they cast and your threat. Players show their guild and, out of combat, item level. Gear says how it compares with what you wear in that slot ("+6 over your chest"); any item shows how many you own in bags and bank. In combat a card keeps only name, difficulty, target, casting and threat. Above the console or at the cursor (/stoa tooltip), and unit cards can hide in combat until you hold Shift.
Trainer
A line under Journey on the console counting the spells and ranks your class trainer would teach you now (Data/TrainerSpells.lua), with their cost; later ranks only once you know the spell, other races' and factions' spells left out. A note when a new level brings some. /stoa trainer, a click on that line, or the Training button beside your class trainer's window opens Training: what's waiting now and what the next two training levels bring, each with its price and the totals. Prices are learned the first time you open a trainer of your class (it lists every spell it will teach) and kept for all your characters. Spells sit under the trainer's own headings (Holy, Protection...) once you've opened it, and every heading folds with a click (kept per character). Right-click a spell you'll never train to skip it on that character.
Mail
Beside the game's own Open All: Take gold (money only, items stay), one line saying what Open All took, and a small arrow inside the name box listing your characters on this realm and the people you mailed lately. Cash on delivery and GM letters are always left for you.
Merchants
Sells grey junk and anything you've marked as junk, and repairs, from guild funds when allowed.

Levelling and gear

Small helps on the road to sixty.

Quest reward
With a choice of rewards, the one that beats what you wear is marked UPGRADE (largest gain), otherwise the one that sells for most is marked VALUE, with a thin accent outline and a small word.
Quest items
A small row beside the Halo with a button for each item your quests ask you to use, filled from your quest log; counts and cooldowns keep up in combat, the row itself changes out of combat (secure buttons)
Gear scores
Gear is scored for your class and role by STOA's own stat weights (Core/Weights.lua), which shift with your level (more Stamina and Spirit early, more hit and crit near 60): the tooltip says UPGRADE +12% over your chest, for Healer; bag slots holding an upgrade show a small UP; the quest reward mark uses the same score. Item level is the fallback when an item can't be scored.
Gear for each spec
Put on an equipment set in a spec and STOA puts it back on whenever you change to that spec, after any fight. A set assigned to a spec in the game's equipment manager wins.
Auto-uprank
When you learn a new spell rank, bar slots holding your old best rank get the new one, out of combat and never with something on your cursor. Lower ranks you put on a bar yourself (for cheap heals) and macros are left alone. A quiet line says what changed. /stoa uprank runs it now.
Restock
Each character keeps a list of things it always carries and how many (candles, ammo, poisons, food and water); any vendor that sells one tops it back up, in whole bundles, then says what it bought. Token and honour items are never bought. Shift as the vendor opens skips it. /stoa restock add <item> 20 or Settings > Comforts.
Junk list
A Junk button on STOA's bag turns on marking: click items to mark or unmark them (it turns off when the bag closes or a fight starts). Marked items show JUNK, sell with the greys, and say so on their tooltip. /stoa junk add|remove <item>, list, clear.
Loot feed
Your last few loots and money above the chat, off by default (the chat lists loot already), fading after about 8 seconds. Gold an hour and loot value this session are lines for At a glance. /stoa loot, /stoa loot reset.
Gathering
Herb and ore node tooltips show the skill they need against yours, with TOO LOW when you're short (English names for now)
Emergency item
One button beside the Halo for your healthstone, else your best healing potion, else your best bandage. In combat it stays in view, quietly (Forever can hide your own health from addons in a fight); out of combat it shows, with LOW, when you're under 40% health.
Characters
Every character writes down its level, money, rest, raid lockouts and what it carries in its bags and bank. Item tooltips count copies on your other characters; /stoa alts lists them all with rest earned since they last played and lockouts on hover.

Comfort and setup

Settings, presets and the little things.

Welcome
Once per character, a short wizard in one window, with a step rail to go back: start by hand, from another character's saved setup or as STOA ships; what each spec plays; theme (house and every class), ornament and colour vision; what a fight does (fold the console with the Halo, keep it open with the Halo, or no Halo, with the calm seconds and group wait under folding); how much (Full, Quiet, Classic feel, Same as Designer); the other addons STOA found (only when there are some: who does each job and STOA's look for Bagnon, Details, Questie, RestedXP); then a summary with ways to try it. Finish always reloads once. Practice combat shows an END COMBAT button that brings the page back. Other addons questions wait for it on a first login.
Presets
Full is how STOA starts. The welcome page offers the others and says what each changes against Full as you hover it: Slim (the console as one plain line: level, health and mana, a word each for Ready, Journey and Company with their pages on a hover card; chat and bars above it on the "Stoa Slim" Edit Mode layout), Minimal (no console: two faint arcs for health and mana out of combat, the Halo in a fight, target plaque and party cards resting faint), Quiet (fewer words: no tooltip details, quest line or At a glance, and the Cooldown Manager's rows hidden on the "Stoa Quiet" Edit Mode layout) or Classic feel (Blizzard's own windows, bags, character sheet, tooltips, top of screen and nameplates, with STOA's console, Halo and frames), or Same as Designer (Full as its designer plays it: Bone theme, painted frames, three bar rows, an 8 second calm, the loot feed in the world, faded nameplates with a target glow, quests accepted for you, cooldowns read from the game's Cooldown Manager, and the designer's Edit Mode layout, added as "STOA Designer"). /stoa preset designer, /stoa editmode designer.
Settings
/stoa opens STOA's settings window: ten pages, a search box, notes on what each setting does, and every /stoa command as a control. Options > AddOns > STOA has a button to it.
Saved setups
Each character's setup (its settings, frame spots, At a glance, combat rows and other per-spec settings, roles and click-casting) is saved account-wide when it logs out. On another character, Load a setup on the backup page, Use a saved setup on the welcome page or /stoa setups; a different class takes the layout and settings but keeps its own roles and click-casting.
Backup
All of STOA's settings as one line of text to keep safe, and a place to paste one back to restore it.
Movers
/stoa unlock (or Move frames in settings) to drag the Halo, target plaque, cast bar, combat bar, party, raid, tank watch, click grid and damage meters. A small bar offers Lock, Reset this layout and a grid; arrow keys nudge the chosen frame 1 (Shift 10); right-click puts one back. Spots are saved per role, so healer and damage layouts differ; older saves still apply.
Snapping
In unlock mode frames snap to the screen's edges and centre and to other frames within 8px, with a faint guide line; hold Shift to drop freely. A frame snapped to another is kept against it, so moving the Halo carries its plaques.
Comforts
Each its own setting, and Shift stops any of it: faster auto loot, a further camera, skip one-option gossip (on by default); accept quests, hand in quests (a choice of reward always waits for you), skip cinematics, quiet red errors in combat (off by default); hide other people's duel results and drunken lines (on); turn the last tracking you chose (Find Minerals, Find Herbs...) back on after login and death (on); decline duels, release in battlegrounds, accept resurrections (off)
Keybinding
/stoa bind (or /stoa kb): hover any action, pet or stance button and press a key, the middle or side mouse buttons or the wheel, with any modifier. Backspace clears, Esc keeps, Discard puts every key back.
Away screen
Going away out of a fight in the open world, the interface steps aside: the camera turns slowly round your character over a band of the console's leather with the wordmark, your name, level, zone, rest, time away and any whispers. Moving, a fight or Back returns. /stoa away shows it.
Tokens
Bone, Tide and Graphite themes, one per class, and Your class (follows the character); spacing and type scale, with a Text size setting (Standard, Larger, Largest) over every size and a floor of 13 screen pixels for the smallest text at any UI scale. Every state carries a word or shape; the themes avoid red against green but are made for looks, so for colour that also holds up for colour-blind eyes use Colour vision.
Colour vision
A setting on top of any theme (also on the welcome page and /stoa vision): Standard, Red-green (protan, deutan), Blue-yellow (tritan) or No colour (lightness only). It swaps only the state colours (warnings, accent, mana, dispel types, shields, health) and shows names in the text colour instead of class colours. Each set is checked by Tools/art/cvd_audit.py: simulated colour blindness (Machado 2009), CIEDE2000 colour difference between states that meet on screen, and WCAG contrast on every theme's panel. Red-green and Blue-yellow keep every pair clearly apart on all themes; No colour keeps them apart as far as lightness alone allows.

Safety and other addons

How STOA keeps out of trouble and plays with others.

Launch check
On the first login with a new game build, checks every spell ID, game function, Blizzard frame and event STOA relies on, and says in one line if a feature is off because something moved. /stoa check (and /stoa report) list the details.
Error guard
STOA records its own errors once each with a count. If one part keeps erroring (a loop), it is switched off until the next reload and STOA says so in chat, so an error can't lock up the screen. /stoa report shows your setup and the errors, ready to copy.
Other addons
Spots a known bag, nameplate, chat, tooltip, bar, minimap, group frame, click-casting or damage meter addon (Details, Skada, Recount) and asks once which should do that job; STOA's part waits meanwhile. A whole UI such as ElvUI or SpartanUI doesn't work beside STOA, and STOA asks you to turn one off.
Leatrix Plus
Works beside Leatrix Plus the same way: where both do a job (faster looting, junk, repairs, quests, gossip, camera, invites, duels, releasing in battlegrounds, accepting resurrections, a square minimap), Leatrix Plus's switch wins while it is on.
STOA's look for other addons
Questie's quest tracker, Bagnon's bag windows and Details' meters can wear STOA's look: offered once (after any "two addons, one job" question), and the addon keeps working exactly as before. Questie gets STOA's type, Bagnon a STOA skin (also in Bagnon's own skin list), Details a STOA skin with no panel, faint bars and STOA's type. Leave it puts their own settings back; /stoa looks asks again. STOA's fonts and a flat bar are in LibSharedMedia as STOA Display, STOA UI and STOA Flat for any addon with a font list.
OneForAll
Works beside OneForAll. Where both do the same small job (fast loot, selling junk, repairs, the loot feed, accepting and handing in quests, camera zoom, moving windows, the chat copy button, a tooltip's target line, upgrade marks, invites and duels), OneForAll's switch wins while it is on and STOA's waits; turn it off there and STOA's comes back. STOA keeps the minimap (OneForAll's square is left to STOA's), the chat rail (OneForAll's chat-hide button joins STOA's column) and waits for OneForAll's welcome page before showing its own. At a glance gains Library Books (found and to find, opens its books panel) and Dungeon guide (opens its dungeon guide). /stoa addons lists what is shared.
DoesItDie
With the DoesItDie addon installed, the target plaque shows what your DoTs will still take off as a bone stretch at the end of its health bar, and a skull slides over the portrait once they'll finish it. DoesItDie does the tracking (through its public API); STOA draws it. It counts as a companion: the welcome page's other addons step and /stoa addons list it. Each part has a setting. STOA's nameplates show the same stretch and a small skull in place of Does It Die's own marker: STOA asks once which you want and sets Does It Die's "Show on nameplates" for you (Settings: Combat, DoT marks on nameplates). The Welcome suggests it to priests, warlocks, druids and hunters who don't have it.
Pawn
With Pawn installed, STOA's upgrade marks give Pawn's answer (its stat scales for your spec) instead of STOA's own weights, so the two never disagree. Where Pawn draws its own marks (its tooltip lines, its bag arrows, its quest reward pick; each a switch in Pawn), STOA leaves that place to Pawn.
For other addons
StoaAPI: add lines to At a glance, hear STOA's state, role and theme changes, and borrow its colours and fonts. See Core/API.lua.
Shared core
Shared helpers every feature leans on: the spellbook read once (best rank of each spell, costs), item worth (level, sell price, against what you wear, junk), your group as a list, mana and the five-second rule, one set of show rules (always, in or out of combat, in a group or raid, alone, with a target, on mouseover, never), settings kept per spec, and unit events asked of the game for just the units that matter.
Core
Event bus, modules, saved settings: each character keeps its own, and a new character starts from the last one's. Junk marks, other addons' choices and trainer data are shared.
